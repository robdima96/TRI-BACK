**Summary:**

## 4. From Eliza to XiaoIce: Challenges and Opportunities with Social Chatbots

**First Author:** Heung-Yeung Shum

**Summary:**

Traces conversational-system evolution from Eliza (1960s) through task-completion programs and personal assistants to modern social chatbots like XiaoIce. **Social chatbots must optimize for both intellectual quotient (IQ) and emotional quotient (EQ), with conversation-turns per session (CPS) proposed as the key success metric.** The authors describe technologies from core chat to visual awareness to skills, and discuss **dynamic emotion recognition and interpersonal responses** as enablers of long, engaging conversations.

**Date:** 2018-01

**Volume:** 19
**Issue:** 1

## 5. Technical Metrics Used to Evaluate Health Care Chatbots: Scoping Review

**First Author:** Alaa Abd-Alrazaq

**Summary:**

Scoping review of **65** studies identifying **27 technical (non-clinical) metrics** for health chatbot evaluation, grouped by what they assess: the chatbot as a whole (e.g., usability, classifier performance, speed), response generation (e.g., comprehensibility, realism), response understanding (e.g., word error rate), and aesthetics. **Survey designs and global usability metrics dominated; the lack of standardization and scarcity of objective measures make cross-study comparison difficult.** The authors recommend **more frequent use of conversation-log-derived metrics** and development of a standardized framework.

**Date:** 2020-06-05
**Publication:** Journal of Medical Internet Research
**Volume:** 22
**Issue:** 6
**Pages:** e18301
**DOI:** 10.2196/18301 <http://doi.org/10.2196/18301>

## 6. Technical Aspects of Developing Chatbots for Medical Applications: Scoping Review

**First Author:** Zeineb Safi

**Summary:**

Scoping review of **45** studies on medical chatbot development identifying **four main modules: text understanding, dialogue management, database layer, and text generation**. The most common technique for text understanding and dialogue management was **pattern matching** (n=18 and n=25 respectively), and the most common text generation approach was **fixed output (n=36)** with very few studies generating original output. **The review notes a recent shift toward machine-learning-based approaches** and calls for research linking clinical outcomes to chatbot development techniques.

**Date:** 2020-12-18
**Publication:** Journal of Medical Internet Research
**Volume:** 22
**Issue:** 12
**Pages:** e19127
**DOI:** 10.2196/19127 <http://doi.org/10.2196/19127>

## 9. Patient-Centered AI Agents for Shared Decision-Making: A Large Language Model-Based Approach for Cataract Management

**First Author:** Wenquan Cheng

**Summary:**

Developed a domain-specific, LLM-powered patient education multi-agent system specifically tailored for cataract management from both open-source and proprietary large language models. System supports personalized, multi-turn dialogue generation, integrates retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with intention recognition, and employs direct preference optimization (DPO) to align responses with expert-level standards and contextual accuracy. **The multi-agent framework showed superior performance compared to traditional LLMs alone: increase in accuracy (12%), reduced rates of hallucination, minimized dissemination of harmful content, improved response comprehensiveness (mean score increase: 0.44, SD 0.10) and understandability (mean increase: 0.19, SD 0.13), and significantly boosted informed decision-making rates to 41.9% from 30.8% with traditional aids (P = 0.02)**.

**Date:** 2025/06/30
**Publication:** Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science
**Volume:** 66
**Issue:** 8
**Pages:** 3945-3945

## 10. ChatGPT and LBP - Evaluating AI-driven patient education in the context of interventional pain medicine

**First Author:** Ahmed Basharat

**Summary:**

Across 25 standardized LBP questions, **clinician reviewers found that "dumbed down" prompts often made ChatGPT's answers less clinically complete, whereas neutral or reference-level prompts preserved accuracy better**. Readability scores still landed above typical health-literacy targets, and mistakes were more often missing information than outright false claims. **Word count and response length did not reliably signal correctness**.

**Date:** 2025-09-01
**Publication:** Interventional Pain Medicine
**Volume:** 4
**Issue:** 3
**Pages:** 100636
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.inpm.2025.100636 <http://doi.org/10.1016/j.inpm.2025.100636>

## 11. Standardizing and Scaffolding Health Care AI-Chatbot Evaluation: Systematic Review

**First Author:** Yining Hua

**Summary:**

Systematic review of 266 records producing the **Health Care AI Chatbot Evaluation Framework (HAICEF)**: a hierarchical framework with **3 priority domains** (safety, privacy, and fairness; trustworthiness and usefulness; design and operational effectiveness), **18 second-level and 60 third-level constructs** covering **271 evaluation questions**. Distribution across domains: design/operational effectiveness 40%, trustworthiness/usefulness 39%, safety/privacy/fairness 21%. **The framework accommodates both patient-facing and back-office chatbot use cases**; planned next steps include prospective validation and Delphi consensus.

**Date:** 2025-11-07
**Publication:** JMIR AI
**Volume:** 4
**Issue:** 1

## Overall synthesis (from Summaries, emphasizing bolded conclusions)

### i) Main findings

**Dialogue system foundations and chatbot architecture.** The literature traces conversational AI from early spoken dialogue systems built around **six core components** (speech recognition, language understanding, dialogue management, external-source communication, language generation, speech synthesis) through to modern social chatbots optimizing for both **IQ and EQ** with conversation-turns per session as a success metric. Medical chatbot development has historically relied on **pattern matching** for text understanding and dialogue management and **fixed output** for response generation, though **a recent shift toward machine-learning-based approaches** is evident. Open-source toolkits such as **Rasa** have lowered barriers by making **ML-based dialogue management accessible to non-specialist developers** with minimal initial training data. Publicly available **dialogue corpora** span many domains, though **transfer learning between datasets and incorporation of external knowledge remain underexplored**.

**Evaluation and metrics.** Evaluation of health chatbots is hampered by **lack of standardization**: across 65 studies, **27 distinct technical metrics** were identified, dominated by **survey designs and global usability scores** with few objective, conversation-log-derived measures. Two frameworks aim to address this: a **systematic scoping review in JAMIA** proposes a structured evaluation framework for AI-powered conversational agents in health interventions, and the more recent **HAICEF framework** organizes **271 evaluation questions** across **3 priority domains** (safety/privacy/fairness, trustworthiness/usefulness, design/operational effectiveness) with **18 second-level and 60 third-level constructs**, accommodating both patient-facing and back-office use cases. An **AI-as-evaluator** approach (three-bot system) **aligned closely enough with human reviewers** to support **rapid, low-risk chatbot testing without recruiting patients**.

**Applied health chatbot performance.** In a **domain-specific, multi-agent LLM system** (cataract management) with retrieval-augmented generation and preference alignment, **performance exceeded traditional LLM-only baselines** on **accuracy (+12%), hallucination reduction, comprehensiveness, understandability, and informed decision-making (41.9% vs 30.8%)**. For **ChatGPT-style answers to standardized LBP questions**, **oversimplified ("dumbed down") prompts reduced clinical completeness** whereas **neutral or reference-level prompts preserved accuracy better**; **response length and word count did not reliably signal correctness**.

### ii) Gaps and opportunities

The literature provides **strong architectural and methodological scaffolding** for building and evaluating health chatbots, but **most work remains outside musculoskeletal and LBP domains**. Key gaps include: the **absence of standardized, reproducible evaluation metrics** across chatbot studies (despite two emerging frameworks, neither has been prospectively validated); the continued dominance of **pattern matching and fixed-output generation** in medical chatbots, lagging behind advances in data-driven and LLM-based dialogue; and the observation that **clinical outcome evidence is rarely linked to chatbot development techniques**. Opportunities include: validating **HAICEF and similar frameworks** in MSK-specific deployments with prospective data and Delphi consensus; adapting **multi-agent + retrieval + preference alignment** architectures to **MSK education, triage, and shared decision-making** with patient-centered outcome measures; leveraging **open-source toolkits** (e.g., Rasa) and available **dialogue corpora** to bootstrap MSK-specific conversational agents; developing **conversation-log-derived metrics** as complements to usability surveys; and testing **prompt and readability guidelines** for LBP/MSK against both **clinician and patient endpoints**, since **readability and length are unreliable proxies for correctness**.
